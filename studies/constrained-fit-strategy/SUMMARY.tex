\begin{answer}
The hours-long ``crawl'' of walled SCETlib-AD fits is not a false minimum: scipy's trust-krylov locks its trust radius in a
limit cycle at the curvature jump of the \relu\ NP damping wall. A curvature-continuous (\Ctwo) ramp on the wall removes it.
From the same stuck point the \Ctwo\ fit reaches the reference minimum in 46 iterations (1.1\,h), while \relu\ is still
430\,NLL above it after 219, and the minimum does not move: $\dalphas=+3.9\times10^{-6}\,\sigma$. \Ctwo\ is now the
\code{NPDampingWall} default. \textbf{Caveat:} one real start was tested (six on a surrogate). The projected-\ptll\
saturated sub-fit is slow for a different reason, the conditioning of the saturated model; a preconditioning test for it
is running. Real data, \alphas\ blinded; shifts in units of the reference fit's $\sigma(\alphas)$.
\end{answer}

\section{Motivation and question}

The walled NP fits (a rabbit regularizer keeps the SCETlib NP parameters in the physically damped region) minimise
with scipy's trust-krylov, and some sat for hours on a slowly falling plateau that looked like a converged fit. The census fit \fit{CENS03} (perturbed start \fit{pert\_002}) had not converged after 390 iterations
\taskref{261006-diagnosis}, and \fit{CMR1A} spent 1.5\,h on a plateau before escaping \logref{2026-10-06}. trust-constr
holds the constraints exactly but did not converge from distant starts: \fit{TCB1} ran 600 iterations (13.6\,h) without
leaving its first barrier stage \taskref{261006-diagnosis}. The question: which recipe holds the physical region exactly
(or to a negligible violation) and reaches the same minimum from any start in predictable time? After the diagnosis it was
narrowed (Luca, 10-06): trust-krylov stays, trust-constr only as a certifier, and a fix at the level of parameters or a
few lines of wall code comes before any reparametrisation. On 10-08 a second question was added: why the projected-\ptll\
saturated sub-fit takes about 5\,h, a cost that toy-calibrated goodness-of-fit tests would pay once per toy.

\section{Setup and method}

\paragraph{Fits.} All fits are on real data with \alphas\ blinded, in the nominal configuration: card~A (2D
$\ptll\times\yll$, Z$\to\mu\mu$), the SCETlib-AD param model on the $|Y|\le2.5$ subset of the \code{pdf62_y35_260921}
cache, the native lattice CS term, $\lnu4\equiv0$, TMD priors pinned. The wall adds $k\sum_iP(x_i)$ to the NLL, with
$x_i$ the violation of physical-damping condition $i$ in that condition's own units (e.g.\ $L_2(Y)=\lambda_2+\delta\lambda_2Y^2\ge0$
at $|Y|=0$ and 2.5, $\lnu2\ge0$, $3\lambda_\infty^2\lambda_4+L_2^3\ge0$), $k=e^{2\tau}$, $\tau=8$, margin 0. Fit names:
\fit{NOMSTIFF} is the converged reference nominal walled fit (NLL 376.6146329237). \fit{CENS03R} is a restart of
\fit{CENS03}, stopped mid-crawl after 260 iterations (4.5\,h) at $+430$\,NLL; its snapshot sits on the
$L_2(|Y|{=}2.5)$ face, $-0.175\,\sigma$ in \alphas\ and $\|\Delta\theta/\sigma\|=29.3$ from \fit{NOMSTIFF}
\taskref{261008-c2-wall-test}. \fit{C2A} (\Ctwo) and \fit{R2A} (\relu) restart from that snapshot at $\tau=8$, side by
side on one node with the same cache subset. \fit{LATB8} is a walled lattice-term fit; \fit{SATB8} (\relu), \fit{SATC2} (\Ctwo) and \fit{SATB8SA} (\relu,
seeded with a previous sub-fit via \code{--saturatedSeed}~\code{all}) are its projected-\ptll\ saturated sub-fits (39 free
bin scales, \code{-m}~\code{Project}~\code{ch0}~\code{ptll}).

\paragraph{Diagnosis.} From the fit logs and a quadratic surrogate (the real data-only Hessian, $3719^2$, the five exact
wall conditions, and an instrumented copy of scipy's trust-region loop). It reproduces two census fits' iteration counts
and \fit{CENS03}'s non-convergence, not \fit{CMR1A}'s crawl \taskref{261006-diagnosis}. Cheap fixes were screened on six
surrogate starts, and the winner run on real fits.

\paragraph{The \Ctwo\ ramp.} The study built a curvature-continuous version of the wall (Fig.~\ref{fig:penalty}). With
$x$ the violation and $d$ the ramp width,
\begin{equation}
  P(x)=\begin{cases}0 & x\le0\\ x^3/(3d) & 0<x<d\\ x^2-dx+d^2/3 & x\ge d\end{cases}
  \qquad\text{against}\qquad P_{\mathrm{relu}^2}(x)=\max(x,0)^2 .
\end{equation}
$P$, $P'$ and $P''$ are continuous; the curvature rises linearly from 0 to the \relu\ value 2 across $[0,d]$. A step that
crosses the face by $\Delta x\ll d$ therefore pays $k\Delta x^3/(3d)$ instead of $k\Delta x^2$. The width is set per
condition, $d=\tilde\delta/s$, with $\tilde\delta=10^{-3}$ in the NP exponent at $b_\mathrm{max}=12.6\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}$
(the largest $b_T$ the cache reaches) and $s$ the exponent per unit of the condition at $b_\mathrm{max}$. That gives
$d=3.15\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$ on $L_2$, $6.3\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$ on \lnu2,
$5.95\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{GeV}^6$ on the cubic condition and $3.97\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{GeV}^4$ on \lnu4\
\taskref{261008-c2-wall-test}. Because the ramp is softer than \relu, the equilibrium at an active face moves outward by up to
$d/2$: at \fit{NOMSTIFF}'s face the prediction was $v=\sqrt{gd/k}=2.40\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$ (data force
$g=16.3$), against $9.2\times10^{-7}$ for \relu, and a loss lower by $1.9\times10^{-5}$. Code: the \code{smooth=} option
in WRemnants \code{wremnants/postprocessing/scetlib_ad/np_damping_wall.py} (each \code{Condition} carries its scale $s$),
tested by \code{scripts/tests/test_np_damping_wall_c2.py} (78 checks, incl.\ gradient and HVP against finite differences
and \code{smooth=relu2} bitwise equal to the old wall).

\section{Results}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{The \Ctwo\ wall removes the crawl on the real fit and does not move the minimum}{The C2 wall removes the crawl on the real fit and does not move the minimum}}

\caveat{one real start, a snapshot of a fit stuck mid-crawl; \fit{C2A} and \fit{R2A} share node load, cache and
settings, and differ only in the wall. \fit{CENS03R} ran three days earlier on the full cache with different stall
flags, and its log ends where \fit{C2A} and \fit{R2A} begin.}

From the \fit{CENS03R} snapshot, \fit{C2A} reaches the \fit{NOMSTIFF} loss to $10^{-6}$ in 46 iterations and 1.11\,h of
minimiser time. \fit{R2A}, the same command with \relu, was still
at $+430$\,NLL after 219 iterations and 1.69\,h and was stopped by hand; \fit{CENS03R} itself had spent 260 iterations
and 4.5\,h there \taskref{261008-c2-wall-test} (Fig.~\ref{fig:crawl}). The log signature agrees: 78\,\% of \fit{R2A}'s
consecutive accepted steps have a gain ratio near 1, the period-2 lock of Sec.~\ref{sec:mech}, against 7.5\,\% for
\fit{C2A}, whose steps double instead as the radius grows (\code{analysis.json}, \taskref{261008-c2-wall-test}).

Every check lands on its prediction (Table~\ref{tab:c2a}). The ramp holds the active face $1.5\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm{GeV}^2$
further out than \relu, and the active-face slope maps that onto $4\times10^{-6}\,\sigma$ in \alphas, which is what is seen.
\textbf{Physics read:} \Ctwo\ changes the minimiser's path, not the measurement. The face is held at the wall with no margin,
at $7.6\times10^{-4}$ of the NP exponent at $b_\mathrm{max}$, inside the $10^{-3}$ tolerance. A \Ctwo--\relu\ loss offset
of $\sim10^{-5}$ is irrelevant for any test statistic.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{The crawl is a limit cycle of scipy's trust-radius rule at a C$^1$ face}{The crawl is a limit cycle of scipy's trust-radius rule at a C1 face}}
\label{sec:mech}

\caveat{the radius-level mechanism comes from the surrogate (nominal configuration only); the real logs record only the
loss, and show the same signature.}

scipy grows the trust radius only if the gain ratio $\rho>0.75$ \emph{and} the step reaches the trust boundary; it shrinks
it if $\rho<0.25$. \relu\ is only C$^1$, so a quadratic model built on the physical side of a face has no wall curvature.
The step from there crosses the face, and the penalty eats about 30\,\% of the predicted gain ($\rho\approx0.71$): no
growth. The model built on the violating side contains the wall; its step lands just back inside, interior, with
$\rho\approx1$: again no growth. The radius freezes (at $3.8\times10^{-6}$ in $\theta$ on the surrogate, scaling as
$1/k$) and the loss falls by a constant amount per iteration (Fig.~\ref{fig:zigzag}). Gain ratios near 1 make up 40--44\,\% of the step
pairs in \fit{CENS03} and \fit{CMR1A}, 1--9\,\% in census fits that converged normally. The
lock-in worsens steeply with the stiffness: on the surrogate, 1 of 5 starts locks in at $\tau=8$, 2 at $\tau=9$, and 3 do
not converge in 20\,000 iterations at $\tau=10$ \taskref{261006-diagnosis}. Conditioning alone is not the cause: with the
active face eliminated exactly, the same Hessian converges from every start in 9--45 iterations. On
the surrogate the \Ctwo\ ramp removes the lock-in from all six starts (18--159 evaluations) \taskref{261006-diagnosis}.

\subsection{The saturated sub-fit is slow because the saturated model is ill-conditioned, not because of the wall}

\caveat{one configuration (\fit{LATB8}); wall times depend on node load, so compare counts. Dense Hessians at three
points, with plain CG standing in for trlib's solve. No remedy has been run yet.}

\fit{SATC2} retraces \fit{SATB8} iteration for iteration (Fig.~\ref{fig:sat}, left): 157 against 153 iterations to the
final loss $-10^{-6}$, 2141 against 2150 Hessian-vector products (HVPs), and the same projected test,
$q=78.4/39$ \taskref{261008-c2-wall-test}\,\taskref{261008-saturated-subfit-diagnosis}. HVPs take 89--91\,\% of the
time, much of it in a few deep, late Krylov solves of up to $\sim$270 HVPs. Each solve gains only 35--40\,\% of what is
left, and the tail below $\Delta q=0.01$ costs 27--56\,\% of the run \taskref{261008-saturated-subfit-diagnosis}.

The cause is the alternative model's geometry (Fig.~\ref{fig:sat}, right). Freeing the \ptll\ shape frees the parameters
it constrained: \alphas\ is 97.9\,\% degenerate with the 39 bin scales ($\rho^2=0.979$). The sub-fit minimum lies
$9.6\,\sigma$ (of the main fit) away in \alphas, with \lnu2, the TNPs and the luminosity moving by 1.6--3.5$\,\sigma$, while
the scales absorb a smooth tilt from 0.908 to 1.106. The Hessian has $\kappa=1.3\times10^{10}$ at the start and
$2.0\times10^{9}$ at the end, the valley is curved, and an exact Newton step diverges even 0.4\,NLL from the minimum.
Profiling the scales analytically ($s_j=N_j/\nu_j$) straightens the valley: along the straight line from start to minimum the loss then falls monotonically, from 16 NLL to 0 \taskref{261008-saturated-subfit-diagnosis}.
\textbf{Physics read:} the answer is robust (the three runs end at the same point to $2.6\times10^{-9}$ in $x$); what is at
stake is the cost per toy.

The remedies, ranked by estimate \taskref{261008-saturated-subfit-diagnosis}: (1)~full-scope spectral preconditioning of
the sub-fit, CLI only, which cuts the CG work per solve 3--4$\times$ on the end Hessian (290$\to$77 HVPs to $10^{-6}$),

certain); (4)~a mid-fit preconditioner rebuild. A stall-window stop (\code{--earlyStopping}
\code{--stallRelTol}) cannot cut the tail: it fires on false plateaus, with $q$ errors of 0.36--20.

\section{Robustness, systematics and caveats}

The real-fit evidence for \Ctwo\ is one start; cold and perturbed starts were covered only on the (quadratic,
nominal-only) surrogate, and the study's benchmark of six starts has not been run. The \Ctwo\ ramp needs a constant scale per condition, so the
interior discriminants of the \code{tanh_6} CS form keep \relu\ by default and can still crawl.

Two problems are untouched. In the no-lattice configuration with \lnu4\ free, the fit \fit{CMR1B} leaks through the
$\lnu4\ge0$ wall to $\gamma_\nu(b_\mathrm{max})=+1.94$ (anti-damped): the data curvature along \lnu4\ there ($7.6\times10^8$)
is 40 times the wall's $2k$, so no practical \relu\ stiffness is exact ($\tau\approx14$, $\kappa\sim10^{12}$)
\taskref{261006-diagnosis}. On the ordinary faces the $\tau=8$ wall leaks only $10^{-4}$--$3\times10^{-4}$ in the exponent at
$b_\mathrm{max}$. In the saturated sub-fit, \lnu4\ also runs onto its face, with a small leak not chased
\taskref{261008-saturated-subfit-diagnosis}.

Fixes that did not work, on the surrogate \taskref{261006-diagnosis}: restart-on-stall either never fires or falls
back into the cycle (up to 662 restarts); \code{--precondition} helped 2 of 5 starts and hurt 3 (built at a feasible start,
it never sees the wall); \code{tf-trust-krylov} has scipy's radius rule (from the code, not run). $\tau$-continuation
($\tau=5$, then $\tau=8$ warm) converged from all six starts but costs two cache loads and was never run on a real fit;
\Ctwo\ superseded it.
